# X-DONUT-001 · L3 · skeleton.svg + pelt.svg

Source: L0 X-DONUT-001 (creature.svg).

## skeleton.svg
Frame only, same pose and same 3/4 camera as the source; the body is a faint
ghost so the bones can be checked against the exterior.

P2 / N1 -> the frame is a mineral lattice: three concentric hoops inside the
shell wall, fourteen radial spokes tying the bore rim to the outer rim, and
collar nodes at every limb root. There is no vertebrae column, no centrum, no
segmental chain; the hoops are rings, not vertebrae. The crest blades root on
the outer hoop, so the dorsal organ is carried by the frame itself.
Q1 -> the bore is a hole in the frame, the same size and in the same place as on
the exterior; the hoops never cross it. Nothing is added or removed.
Q2/Q3/Q4 -> four limb bones: two thick leg bones with a knee joint and a foot
plate, two thinner arm bones with an elbow and a three-claw rake. Joints align
with the exterior knuckles of the source. Count is unchanged: 4.
Q5 -> no eyes: the frame carries no orbit or socket on the front face.
F3 -> the crest sockets sit on the outer hoop, six of them, matching the blades.

## pelt.svg
The skin as ONE continuous piece, unfolded flat and stretched: dorsal edge up,
ventral edge down, flanks left and right. Every hole is punched through the same
single sheet.

PELT: one continuous sheet. Seven radial seams run from the bore collar out to
the rim, every one of them stitched with stone thread. The seam that opens the
sheet (the cut that lets a ring lie flat) runs along the upper left edge and is
drawn as a broken cut line. The sheet is a single filled outline with holes cut
out of it, not a collage.

Holes in the sheet, in order of size:
1. Q1 bore — the largest hole, centre of the sheet, ringed by a second dashed
   ring: that ring is the tunnel lining folded flat. The bore is a tunnel
   boundary, not a marking; the lining ring is why the hole is wider than the
   mouth seen on the creature.
2. three feeding openings — a row of three on the ventral band, middle largest.
3. four limb sockets — two wide ones low (legs, with a thickened socket rim) and
   two smaller ones on the flanks (manipulators).
4. six crest sockets — narrow slits along the dorsal edge, burnt at the rim.

P2 -> no fur, no scale, no membrane: the sheet is stony hide with stretch marks
and a darker ventral side, seams stitched with stone thread.
F3 -> the crest sockets are the hottest points on the sheet, and their rims are
scorched.
Q5 -> no eye holes: no paired round opening anywhere on the sheet.
Q6 -> exactly three feeding holes.
Q2/Q3/Q4 -> exactly four limb sockets: 2 + 2.
RECONCILE -> T-01: the two leg sockets are wider and sit on the ventral band;
the two manipulator sockets are smaller and sit on the flanks. 2 + 2 = 4.

No cutaway, no exploded view, no separated pieces: the sheet is one piece with
holes, seen from outside.
